# Messages

Sometimes the thing you need to say doesn't belong on a task.

**Messages** are private conversations between two people, or a few. They're end-to-end encrypted: the people in the conversation can read them, and nobody else can, including a community admin, whoever runs your server, and us.

Everything else in Initiative has comments, so the conversation about a piece of work sits on that work, where the next person finds it and search can turn it up again. That matters, because a group chat is where somebody pastes the thing that should have been a document, and six months later everyone is scrolling for it, and Jenny has left, and somebody is looking at the printer in a way the printer has done nothing to deserve. Messages are for the quiet word on the side.

Open them from **My Messages** in your [own sidebar](your-space.md) (select the Initiative logo at the top of the community rail to get there), or the speech bubble in the bar along the bottom of a phone. Conversations are listed with the latest at the top, a dot marks one with something unread, and **Search** above them finds one by name.

Messaging is for people aged 16 and over, so if Initiative asks, answer with **Confirm my age** under **My Settings → Privacy**. If the page says **Direct messages are off here**, whoever runs your server has switched messaging off, and your conversations are kept for whenever it comes back.

## Choose who can ask to message you { #who-can-reach-you }

Nobody can start a conversation with you out of the blue. Somebody first asks to message you, and nothing is sent until you accept. This setting decides who's allowed to ask.

1. Open **My Settings → Privacy**.
2. Under **Direct messages**, pick who can ask:

| Setting | Who can ask |
|---|---|
| **Private** | Nobody. |
| **My communities** | People you share a community with. A switch for each community appears underneath, so you can leave one out. |
| **Anyone** | Anybody on Initiative, including people in none of your communities who know your whole handle. |

It saves as soon as you pick. Whatever you choose, people you're [connected](#connect-with-someone) with can always message you, and anybody who knows your whole handle can ask to connect, which you're free to decline.

New accounts start on whatever whoever runs your server set as the default, and the default that ships is **Private**. Changing that default only affects accounts made afterwards.

The same section holds **Tell people when their messages reach you**. On, the people you talk to see when a message arrived on one of your devices and when you read it. Off, they see neither, and you see theirs either way.

## Start a conversation

1. In **My Messages**, select **+** (**Start a conversation**).
2. Type a name, or somebody's whole handle with its number, like `sam#1234`.
3. Pick the person. Their conversation opens.
4. If you haven't talked before, select **Ask to message**.
5. Once they accept, write in **Write a message**.
6. Select **Send**.

The picker lists the people in each of your communities, with **Show more** under each for the next batch. A whole handle is the only way to find somebody in none of your communities. Somebody who can't be reached is listed as **Not reachable**, with nothing to select.

Your request carries no text. They see that you'd like to talk and nothing else, and the conversation opens for both of you when they select **Accept**. If they decline, the request quietly goes away, and either of you can ask again another day.

If they've never opened Messages, they have nowhere to receive one yet, and Initiative says so instead of pretending the message went.

## Start a conversation with a few people { #talking-to-a-few-people-at-once }

A conversation with three people works the way one with two does: everyone on it agreed to be there, and it's the people on it, with no name or owner.

1. In **My Messages**, select **+** (**Start a conversation**).
2. Find the first person.
3. Select the **+** beside their name.
4. Do the same for everyone else.
5. Select **Start a group with** (however many) **people**.

Everyone you add is asked, even people who've messaged each other for years, and nothing reaches them until they say yes. They see who else is on it, under **You have been asked to join a group**, and choose **Join** or **No thanks**. Somebody who said no can be asked again another time.

A few things to know while you're picking:

- **Everyone has to be able to reach everyone**, not only you. If two of the people you picked can't message each other yet, Initiative says so while you're still choosing. A [connection](#connect-with-someone) between them, or a wider [privacy setting](#who-can-reach-you), fixes it.
- **At most 40 people.** By then the conversation probably wants to be a post.
- **Pick the same people again** and you land back in the conversation you already have.
- **The list is set when you start it.** Wanting one more person means starting a new conversation, which everybody agrees to afresh, so nobody is ever reading a conversation they didn't agree to be in.

### Catching up after you join

The server keeps no copy of a conversation, so there's nothing on it to catch you up from. When you join, one of the others sends you the conversation from their own device, as much as they still have. It may arrive a minute after you do, or, if everyone's laptop is shut, whenever one of them opens it.

## Answer a request

Requests waiting on you sit at the top of **My Messages** under **Requests**, and under **Pending** in **My Settings → Privacy**. You get a notification for each one too.

1. Find the request.
2. Select **Accept** or **Decline**.

A request you sent shows there as well, with **Cancel** beside it if you change your mind.

## Reply to, edit, react to or remove a message

Point at a message (or tap it on a phone) and a small bar appears above it.

- **React**: select the emoji button and pick one. Select it again to take your reaction back.
- **Reply**: select **Reply**, write your answer, and send it. Your message quotes the one you answered.
- **Edit** one of your own: select **Edit**, change it, and send. It shows **(edited)**. Press Escape to stop without changing it.
- **Remove** one of your own: select **Remove**, then **Remove** again to confirm.

Removing takes the message off your device and asks the other devices to take it off theirs, where it reads **Message removed**. A message somebody already read has been read, though, and no button can unsay it.

Under your own messages, **Sent**, **Delivered** and **Read** say how far each one got, for anybody who leaves **Tell people when their messages reach you** on.

## Connect with someone

A connection is a two-way link between your account and theirs. Once they accept, the two of you can always message each other, whatever your privacy settings say, and even if you stop sharing a community or never shared one.

1. Open **My Settings → Privacy**.
2. Under **Connections**, type their whole handle, number included (`name#1234`).
3. Select **Send request**.

You can also send one from the **+** in My Messages (type their whole handle and select **Connect with** under it), or from the actions menu beside their name on their profile, in a member list or in your conversations, with **Connect**. They're told, and you're told when they accept.

### Remove a connection

1. Find them under **Connections** in **My Settings → Privacy**, or open the actions menu beside their name.
2. Select **Remove** (or **Remove connection** in the menu).
3. Confirm.

If nothing else lets you message each other (a community you share, or both of you being open to messages), your conversations end with it. The confirmation says so before you decide.

## Ignore someone { #ignoring-someone }

Ignoring is the firm answer, for when the polite one hasn't worked.

1. Open the actions menu beside their name: on their profile, in a member list, or in your conversations.
2. Select **Ignore**.

Somebody you ignore stops reaching you: no notifications when they mention you, reply to you or react to you, and nothing they send arrives, including messages, message requests and connection requests. They aren't told. You both keep seeing each other's work in communities you share, and you can both use every tool as normal, because ignoring is about contact rather than about work.

Everybody you ignore is listed under **Ignored accounts** in **My Settings → Privacy**. **Ignore by handle** there takes a whole handle, for somebody whose profile you'd rather not go looking for. **Stop ignoring** puts everything back exactly as it was, since nothing was deleted.

## Your messages live on your devices { #your-messages-live-on-your-devices }

Read this before you rely on Messages, because it works differently from everything else in Initiative and it surprises people.

A message is delivered to the devices of the people in the conversation and then deleted from the server. Nothing waits in the middle for a new device to catch up from, so:

- **Each device keeps its own copy** of your conversations.
- **A new device starts empty**, unless one of your other devices sends it your history when you [add it](#read-your-messages-on-a-new-device).
- **Signing out of a browser deletes the messages in it.** That's what you want on a library computer, and not what you want if you meant to read them again.
- **The phone and desktop apps keep your messages when you sign out**, each account's separately, so they're there when you sign back in. The apps are the place to keep the conversations that matter.

Messages need a secure connection and a browser that can run the encryption. If a browser can't, Initiative says so, and the apps always can.

## Read your messages on a new device

When you sign in somewhere new, the new device asks you to verify it from one you already use. Keep both open side by side.

1. On a device you already use, open **My Messages**. It shows **New device signed in**.
2. To give the new device your message history, tick **Send it my message history**.
3. Select **Verify**.
4. Check that both screens show the same four pictures, in the same order.
5. Select **They match** on both devices.

The pictures are different every time, so there's nothing to write down. If they don't match, select **They don't match**: nothing changes, and you can try again. If you don't recognize the device at all, select **Not mine, remove it** and it's taken off your account.

When somebody you talk to adds or replaces a device, your conversation says so in one quiet line where it happened. You don't need to do anything about it; it's only there so the change isn't a surprise.

## Notifications about messages

You're told who messaged you and how many times, never what they said, because nothing outside your own devices can read it. A burst of messages arrives as one notification rather than twenty. Turn them on or off with the **Messages and connections** row in [My Settings → Notifications](notifications.md#choose-what-you-hear-about).

## What community admins can see

Messages belong to the people in them. A community admin can open everything in their community, and that stops at a private conversation: messages aren't in community exports or search, and there's nothing in them for anybody else to moderate.

You decide who can reach you, and you can decline a request or ignore an account without needing anyone's help. If an account is behaving badly, report it. See [Reporting a problem](../security/reporting-a-problem.md), and [Private messages](../security/private-messages.md) for exactly what the encryption covers.
