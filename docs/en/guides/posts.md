# Posts

**Posts** is an initiative's board, where it says things out loud: the gym is booked, practice moved to Thursday, and Sam is running the snack table this year, so everyone be nice to Sam.

None of that is a task, and it isn't really a document either. It's a notice: read once, acted on, then kept as the record of what was said and when. Pinning it to a corkboard worked fine for a century, right up until half the group stopped walking past the corkboard.

## Turn on posts

Posts are off in a new initiative. An initiative manager switches them on:

1. Open **Initiative settings**.
2. On the **Details** tab, find **Tools**.
3. Turn on **Posts**.

A **Posts** tab appears in the initiative. Turning it off later hides the board without deleting anything.

## Post a notice

Posting takes a role in the initiative that can create posts. If you don't see **New Post**, ask an initiative manager.

1. Open the initiative's **Posts** tab.
2. Select **New Post**.
3. Type a **Headline**.
4. Write the notice under **Post**.
5. Optional: **Add a poll** (see [Ask a question with a poll](#ask-a-question-with-a-poll)).
6. Optional: open **Advanced options** to choose who sees it.
7. Select **New Post**.

The notice goes to the top of the board, under anything pinned, and everyone it's shared with is told (see [Choose who sees a notice](#choose-who-sees-a-notice)).

The body is written in the same editor as a text document, with a shorter toolbar and the full `/` menu, up to 10,000 characters. Pictures, links and [smart chips](editor.md#smart-chips) all work, so "the field trip forms are due" can carry a chip showing that task's status long after you wrote it. A notice can have no body at all: a headline and a poll is a perfectly good notice.

If the initiative's notices [require](layouts.md#required-fields) tags or metadata, the dialog asks for them under the editor and waits until they're in.

## Choose who sees a notice

A notice goes to everyone in the initiative unless you say otherwise, under **Advanced options** when you write it or on its **Access** tab later. Only the people it's shared with can see it or are told about it.

That's the useful bit: a notice shared with five people interrupts five people. Posting something the costume team needs doesn't ring a bell for the other ninety.

People hear about a new notice in the bell, and by email or push if they've left those on, under **New in your communities** in their [notification settings](notifications.md#choose-what-you-hear-about). You aren't told about your own.

## Schedule a notice for later

It's handy for the Sunday reminder you'd rather write on Thursday, while the details are fresh.

1. Write the notice as above.
2. Under **Schedule**, pick a date and time.
3. Select **Schedule Post**.

Until then it's a draft: nobody sees it, it's in no count and no search result, and nobody has been told. People who can edit it see it on the board with a dashed edge and **Scheduled for** its time.

To change the time, open the notice and change **Publishes at**. To put it up early, select **Post now** on its card or page. Once a notice is up it stays up, since everyone it was announced to has already been told.

## Edit a notice

Editing a notice takes edit access to it, which its author has.

1. Open the notice from the board.
2. Change the words in the body.
3. Select **Save**.

Click the headline to rename it. Leave with unsaved changes and Initiative asks first.

## Ask a question with a poll

A notice can ask a question with up to ten choices: which night suits, who's driving, whether we're really doing the raffle again.

1. In **New Post**, select **Add a poll**. On a posted notice, open it and select **Add a poll**.
2. Optional: type a **Question**. Leave it empty and the headline is the question.
3. Type at least two **Choices**. **Add a choice** adds another.
4. Pick the settings you want (below).
5. Post the notice, or select **Save** on a posted one.

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Pick several** | People can choose more than one answer. |
| **Hide results until voted** | The numbers stay hidden until somebody answers, so early answers don't sway later ones. |
| **Anonymous** | Shows the totals but not who chose what. |
| **Closes** | Nobody can answer or change their answer after this. Leave it empty and the poll **Stays open**. |

## Answer a poll

Click a choice. That's your answer, and the totals update.

Click it again to take it back, or select **Clear my answer**. With **Pick several**, click each choice you want. You can change your mind until the poll closes.

**Who voted** shows who chose what and who hasn't answered yet, the author included, since writing a question doesn't stop you answering it. An anonymous poll has no such list, and a poll hiding its results shows it only once you've answered.

## Change a poll people have answered

1. Open the notice.
2. Select **Edit poll**.
3. Make the change.
4. Select **Save**.

Once anybody has answered, the choices are fixed: a vote cast for "Tuesday" must not quietly become a vote for whatever replaced it. You can still reword the question and move the closing time. **Anonymous** and **Pick several** can be switched on afterwards but never off, for the same reason. **Remove poll** takes the whole poll off the notice.

## Pin a notice to the top

Pinning is for initiative managers and community admins, so the top of the board isn't just whoever posted last and felt strongest about it.

1. Select the pin on the notice's card, or **Pin to top** on its page.
2. Optional: on the pinned notice, select **Add an end date**.
3. Pick the date under **Stop pinning on**.
4. Select **Save**.

The end date is the part worth knowing about. A notice about Sunday should stop shouting on Monday, and if it's relying on somebody remembering to unpin it, it won't. With a date set, the notice drops back into the board by its own age when the date passes. **Change end date** and **Remove end date** are on the pinned notice; **Unpin** takes the pin off by hand.

## See who's read a notice

A notice marks itself read once it's been on your screen for a second, long enough to be reading it rather than scrolling past. There's nothing to tick off.

Each notice shows **Read by** and a count. Select it to see the list in two tabs: who has read it, and who it's waiting on. "Waiting on" means the people it was shared with, so a board of a hundred where a notice went to five isn't ninety-five people ignoring you. Anyone who can see the notice can see the list. Your own notices never count as unread for you.

- **Mark unread** puts one back in your pile, for the thing you'll deal with properly on Monday.
- To see only what's left, open **Filters**, then set **Read** to **Unread only**.

## Find an older notice

Boards get long. The rail down the side of the board marks every month that has notices, longer where the month was busy. Drag along it (or click a month) and the board jumps there, with the month named as you go.

A line at the top says **Showing from** that month, with **Back to latest** to return. Pins step aside while you're back there, because a pin says what matters now and you're reading what mattered then.

**Filters** also searches the board by headline and content, and narrows it by tag.

## React to a notice or comment on it

Under each notice, select **React with 👍** or **Add a reaction** to react, the same way as on a [comment](comments.md#react-to-a-comment). Anyone who can see the notice can react, and the people following it (its author among them) hear about it.

To discuss it, select **Be the first to comment** (or the comment count) under the notice. That opens the notice's page, with its thread at the bottom.

## Turn off comments or reactions on a notice

Every notice has its own thread and its own reactions, and the notice that's purely an announcement doesn't need either. Whoever can edit the notice can switch them off.

1. Open the notice.
2. Select **Settings**.
3. On the **Details** tab, turn off **Comments**, **Enable reactions**, or both.

What's already there is kept, ready for when you switch them back on. See [Comments & reactions](comments.md).

## Share, archive, export or delete a notice

A notice is shared like everything else (**Viewer**, **Editor** or **Owner**, or open to the whole initiative) on the **Access** tab of its **Settings**. See [Sharing projects & files](../sharing/sharing-projects-and-files.md).

The **Advanced** tab has **Archive**, **Export** (as an **Importable file (.json)**, for whoever can also delete it) and **Delete**, which sends it to the trash. **More actions** › **Import from file** on the **Posts** tab brings an exported notice back.

How a notice's page is laid out is set once for the whole initiative; see [Layouts](layouts.md).
