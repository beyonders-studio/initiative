# Communities

A **community** is your group's own space in Initiative: the rec league, the community theater, the family group that plans Thanksgiving in March. Everything the group makes lives inside it, and only its members can see in. You can belong to as many as you like, and they never mix.

This page is about getting into communities and getting around inside them. If you're the one running a community, [Running a community](running-a-community.md) has the settings, invites and roles.

## Switch between communities

The **community rail** is the narrow strip down the far left of the screen. Every community you're in has an icon there, and the highlighted one is where you are now. Select another icon and the whole app moves over with you: the sidebar, the initiatives, the lot.

The **Initiative logo** at the top of the rail takes you [home](your-space.md), where everything assigned to you in every community sits on one page.

!!! tip "Two communities at once"
    Each browser tab keeps its own community, so the day job can sit in one tab and the PTA bake sale in the other, and neither will find out about the other.

### Put your communities in your own order

Your order is yours alone. Rearranging your rail changes nobody else's.

1. Select **Expand community list**, the double arrow near the bottom of the rail.
2. Select **Reorder**.
3. Drag the communities into the order you want.
4. Select **Done**.

The expanded list also shows each community's banner, description, categories and member count, which helps once you're in enough of them that the icons start to look alike. On a phone, press and hold a community's icon and choose **Reorder communities** to start.

### Use the community menu

Right-click a community's icon on the rail, or press and hold it on a phone, for a short menu:

| Item | What it does |
|---|---|
| **View initiatives** | Opens the community's front page. |
| **View members** | Opens its **Members** page. |
| **Copy community ID** | Copies the community's number, for when whoever runs your server asks which one you mean. |
| **Leave community** | See [Leave a community](#leave-a-community). |

People who run the community see a few more items there, such as **Invite members** and **Community Settings**.

## See what's happening on the community front page { #the-community-front-page }

Opening a community lands you on its front page. Its name, description and location sit on the banner, along with how many members it has and how many are online right now. Select the member count to see everyone.

Under the banner is what people have been up to this week, one card per thing, saying who did what to it and when. Select a card to open the thing. You only ever see cards for things you can open.

Three circles sit above the cards:

| Circle | Shows |
|---|---|
| **All** | Everything that changed this week. It opens first. |
| **Yours** | What happened to your things: what you made, what you're assigned to or going to, and what you changed or commented on. |
| **Comments** | The latest comments from anywhere in the community, with who said what and where. |

The cards aren't strictly newest first. Something recent rises, so does something lots of people are busy with, and so does anything of yours, like the task you're on or a project you subscribed to. Your own changes never make a card, because you were there. Reactions, reading a post and voting in a poll don't make cards either. Anything that changed since your last visit is marked **New**, and each device keeps its own count.

Back from a week off and facing forty cards? Where [AI features](../account/ai-features.md) are switched on, **Catch me up** writes you a short summary of what changed since you were last here.

Further down are **Initiatives**, split into **Your initiatives** and **Open to join** (see [Join an initiative](initiatives.md#join-an-initiative)), and **Coming up**: everything in the community with a date in the next seven days.

To see every project, file or dashboard that has reached you, here and in your other communities, open [My Tools](your-space.md).

## Join a community with an invite link

Most people arrive this way: somebody who runs a community sends a link. It works whether or not you have an account yet.

1. Open the link.
2. Select **Accept invite**. If you're not signed in, select **Sign in to accept**, or **Register using this invite** to make an account first.
3. Select **Continue to Initiative**.

The community's icon appears on your rail. If its admins picked an initiative to welcome newcomers, you're already in it.

!!! note "If your invite link doesn't work"
    Invite links can run out after a number of days or a number of uses. That's the link, not you. Ask whoever sent it for a fresh one, which takes them about four seconds.

## Find a community to join { #finding-a-community-to-join }

Some communities list themselves in the **community directory**, where anyone signed in can find them and join without an invite. You'll find **Join a community** at the bottom of the rail, just under the **+**, on servers that run a directory. No button means your server doesn't run one, so every community there is invite-only. Whoever runs the server decides that (see [Configuration](../running-a-server/configuration.md)).

1. Select **Join a community** at the bottom of the rail.
2. Type in **Search communities**, or pick a category in the sidebar.
3. Select **Join** on the community's card.
4. If you're asked your date of birth, enter it and select **Confirm and join**.

You're a member straight away, with no approval to wait for, and the app takes you inside. A community you're already in says **Open** on its card instead.

Search covers names and places, so "Seattle" finds communities in Seattle, and typing a country's name finds everything in that country, in whatever language you type it. What you searched and which category you picked are part of the page's address, so a filtered directory is a link you can send somebody.

!!! info "Why it asks your age"
    On servers that check ages, every account gives its date of birth once and is never asked again. A listed community is open to anyone signed in, so joining one, from the directory or by invite, takes being 16 or older. Communities that aren't listed have no age limit. Your date of birth is kept encrypted, used only to check age limits, and never shown to anyone. See [Data and compliance](../security/data-and-compliance.md).

### Show the communities near you first

1. Select **Near me** above the cards.
2. Start typing where you are and pick it from the list.
3. Select **Show nearby first**.

The directory then lists communities within about 60 miles (100 km) of you first, whichever side of a border they're on, then the rest of your country, then communities that haven't said where they are, then everywhere else. It hides nothing; it only sorts. This device remembers your place for next time, and the **×** beside it (**Stop sorting by location**) forgets it.

## See who's in a community

1. Select the member count on the community's banner. You can also right-click its icon on the rail and choose **View members**.
2. Type a name or handle in **Filter members** to find somebody.

Admins and superadmins wear a badge. Each person's row offers the same ways to reach them as their profile does.

## Choose your name in a community { #your-name-in-a-community }

Everyone shows as their handle, like `jordan#1234`, until they pick a **display name**: the name people see instead, in one community only. You can be "Coach Jordan" in the soccer league and plain `jordan#1234` at the HOA, and neither one knows about the other.

1. Open the community's **Members** page.
2. Select **Set your display name**.
3. Type the name in **Display name**.
4. Select **Save**.

Your display name shows wherever that community shows people, and members can search for it. To go back to your handle, clear the box and save. Your community's admins can set or clear it for you too, which is handy when four people are called Sam.

## Leave a community

Anyone can leave, guests included.

1. Right-click the community's icon on the rail, or press and hold it on a phone.
2. Select **Leave community**.
3. Select **Leave Community**.

You lose access to everything in the community, and it drops off your rail. Anything you owned there stays behind with nobody owning it, until an admin claims it. Everyone else carries on, which is either a relief or mildly wounding depending on the day you're having.

If you're the community's only superadmin, you'll be asked to make somebody else a superadmin first, so the place isn't left with nobody holding the keys. If you're the last person in it, you can go. To come back later, you need a new invite, or a trip through the directory if the community is listed.

## When a community is read-only or suspended

Whoever runs the server can restrict a community. In a **read-only** community a banner across the top says so: you can still open and read everything, but nothing can be changed. A **suspended** community doesn't open at all until the suspension is lifted, and opening it shows who to contact, with **Ask for help** where the server takes help requests (see [Getting help](help-and-feedback.md)). It's between the community's admins and whoever runs the server, and nothing you did.
