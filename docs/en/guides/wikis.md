# Wikis

A **wiki** is what your group knows, written down somewhere it can be found again: how the concession stand cash box works, what the board decided about dogs, which of the three spreadsheets is the real one.

Every group has this knowledge, and most keep it in a person. That works beautifully until the person is on vacation and it turns out nobody else knows the code for the supply closet.

A wiki is a set of pages that file under each other and link to each other. Make one wiki per body of knowledge ("Team handbook", "World bible", "How we run the fall festival") and let the topics be its pages.

## Turn on wikis

Wikis are off in a new initiative. An initiative manager switches them on:

1. Open **Initiative settings**.
2. On the **Details** tab, find **Tools**.
3. Turn on **Wikis**.

A **Wikis** tab appears in the initiative. Turning it off later hides the wikis without deleting anything.

## Make a wiki

Making one takes a role in the initiative that can create wikis. If you don't see **New Wiki**, ask an initiative manager.

1. Open the initiative's **Wikis** tab.
2. Select **New Wiki**.
3. Type a **Name** and, if you like, a **Description** of what it covers.
4. Optional: open **Advanced options** to choose who can see it.
5. Select **New Wiki**.

Everyone in the initiative can read it to begin with.

## Open a wiki and get around it

Open a wiki from the **Wikis** tab and it takes over the sidebar: your usual navigation slides away and the wiki's pages take its place, with an arrow at the top to climb back out. A wiki is read by moving around it, so its shape stays in front of you the whole time.

- Click a page to read it.
- The arrow beside a page opens what's under it: the pages filed there, then the headings written on it. **Expand all** at the top of the list opens everything.
- **Search this wiki**, above the list, finds a page by its title.
- **Previous** and **Next**, at the foot of each page, walk through the wiki in order.

The wiki opens on its home page. If nobody has chosen one, it opens on the first page in the list.

## Add a page

1. Open the wiki.
2. Select **New page** at the bottom of the page list.
3. Type the title where it says "What this page is about".
4. Write the page.

The page is written in the same editor as a text document, with the same toolbar, so [Writing & formatting](editor.md) covers everything you can put on it. Several people can write on the same page at once, each with their own cursor, and it saves as you go.

A new page lands at the end of the list. To file it under another page, [drag it](#file-a-page-under-another-page).

## Edit a page

Writing in a wiki needs edit access to it.

1. Open the page.
2. Select **Edit this page** (the pencil) above it.
3. Make your changes.
4. Select **View as a reader** to go back to reading.

On a computer you can also hover the page in the list, open **Page actions** and choose **Edit this page**.

## File a page under another page

Pages nest, so "Concession stand" can have "Cash box", "Stock" and "The register that hates everyone" filed under it. Two levels is usually plenty; three is fine.

1. Drag the page in the list.
2. Drop it on the middle of another page to file it under that page, or on the top or bottom edge of a page to put it beside.

A page can't be dropped inside itself or inside anything filed under it, which is the one move the list won't let you make. Pages sit in the order you drag them into, unless the wiki is set to sort them (see [Change how a wiki reads](#change-how-a-wiki-reads)).

## Link pages together

Links are what make a wiki more than a folder of pages.

1. On a page, type `#` and a few letters of another page's title (or `#wiki-page:` to look only at pages).
2. Pick the page.

Then look at the page you linked to: its **Connections** lists your page under **Mentioned in**. You never maintain a list of what points where; you write a link because it made sense in that sentence, and the map builds itself from the links everybody happened to write.

It isn't only pages. Link the cash box page from a task about counting the cash box, and the page tells you the task exists. Titles get rewritten constantly, and links keep working through every rename. See [Mentions & links](mentions-and-links.md).

**Connections** (the button above the page) sits beside the page on a wide screen and opens over it on a phone. A wiki page shows it as a **List** to start with.

## Keep a page as a draft

A draft is how you leave something half-finished in a wiki people read. Only people who can edit the wiki see it, marked **Draft**, and it stays out of exports.

1. Hover the page in the list.
2. Open **Page actions**.
3. Choose **Mark as draft**.

When it's ready, select **Publish** above the page, or choose **Publish** in **Page actions**.

## Duplicate a page

1. Hover the page in the list.
2. Open **Page actions**.
3. Choose **Duplicate**.

A copy called "(Copy)" appears beside it, with its tags, links and metadata. The pages filed under it stay where they are, so copying "Concession stand" gives you one page to rewrite rather than a second concession stand.

## Choose the home page

Make the home page a short contents page: three sentences on what this wiki is, and links to the four places most people are actually going.

1. Hover the page in the list.
2. Open **Page actions**.
3. Choose **Set as home page**.

The home page wears a house in the list. You can also choose it under **Home page** in the wiki's settings, on the **Details** tab.

## Make every new page start the same way

When a wiki is two hundred entries of the same shape (recipes, characters, venues), nominate one page and every new page begins as a copy of it.

1. Hover that page in the list.
2. Open **Page actions**.
3. Choose **Use as the template for new pages**.

The menu then says **New pages start from this one**. To change or clear it, use **New pages start from** in the wiki's settings, on the **Details** tab, and pick a page or **An empty page**.

## Put a file in a wiki

Files you already have can sit in a wiki's page list too: the PDF, the spreadsheet, the whiteboard. The safety plan can live under "Health and safety", where people will actually look for it.

1. Select **Add a file** at the bottom of the page list.
2. Pick the file.

Nothing is copied. The file keeps its own address and its own sharing, opens in the wiki the way it opens anywhere, drags like a page, and can sit in another wiki too. If the wiki holds files you can't open, the list says how many, with **Request access** to ask for them all at once.

To take one out, hover it, open **Page actions** and choose **Remove from this wiki**. The file stays in Files.

## Add metadata or comments to a page

- **Metadata**, above the page, holds the initiative's [metadata](initiatives.md) for this page.
- **Comments**, above the page, opens the page's thread. See [Comments & reactions](comments.md).

## Change how a wiki reads

Anyone who can edit the wiki can change these. Open the gear at the top of the page list (**Wiki settings**), then the **Reading** tab:

| Setting | What it changes |
|---|---|
| **Page order** | **The order you put them in** (drag them), **By title, A to Z**, or **Most recently edited first**. |
| **Page layout** | **Full width** for tables and screenshots, or **Reading width** for prose. |
| **Contents depth** | How many heading levels the page list shows under a page: 2, 3 or 4. |
| **Show when a page changed** | Shows when each page was last updated. A handbook people act on needs it; a world bible doesn't. |
| **Show what links to a page** | The **Connections** list on every page. Turn it off for a wiki nobody cross-references. |
| **Accent color** | Marks the wiki, so two open at once are easy to tell apart. |

## Share a wiki

A wiki is shared as one thing, from the gear's **Access** tab: people who can view read the whole thing, and people who can edit add and change pages. Pages aren't shared separately, because a handbook with three pages missing isn't a handbook. See [Sharing projects & files](../sharing/sharing-projects-and-files.md).

## Download a copy of a wiki

Exporting is for whoever can also delete the wiki.

1. Open the gear (**Wiki settings**).
2. Select the **Advanced** tab.
3. Under **Export**, select **Export**.
4. Pick a format.

**PDF (.zip)**, **Markdown (.zip)** and **Word (.zip)** give you the whole wiki as one document: every page under a heading with its title, nested as deep as the page sits, drafts left out. **Importable file (.zip)** keeps every page, the tree, the home page and the files kept in it.

Either way the download is a zip: the wiki, plus the files filed in it, each in the format that suits it (spreadsheets as Excel, uploads as themselves). A file you couldn't export on its own stays behind. An initiative with **Keep content in this initiative** switched on exports nothing.

To bring an importable file back in, here or on another server, open the **Wikis** tab, select **More actions** › **Import from file** and pick the zip. The files go back under the same pages.

## Delete a page and get it back

1. Hover the page in the list.
2. Open **Page actions**.
3. Choose **Delete page**.
4. Select **Delete page** to confirm.

The page goes to the trash and takes the pages filed under it, because putting away a section means the whole section. Restore it from your trash (**My Settings** › **Trash**) until your community's retention period ends.

To delete a whole wiki (its owner can), open the gear, then the **Advanced** tab, and select **Delete**.

## What to put in a wiki

Roughly: would somebody ask this in the group chat, and would somebody else have to remember the answer?

- **How something works.** The cash box, the volunteer schedule, the thing with the printer.
- **What was decided, and why.** The decision gets remembered and the reason evaporates by March, so write the why down.
- **The list somebody keeps in their head.** Suppliers, which door sticks, the passwords for things that aren't secret.
- **The onboarding you do out loud**, in full, every time, to each new person.

Anything with a date and a person attached is a task, and a conversation belongs in the comments on the thing it's about.
