# Glossary

Initiative takes a handful of perfectly ordinary words and gives them specific jobs. These are the ones where guessing from the everyday meaning will lead you somewhere wrong.

Everything else is explained on the page that owns it, and ++cmd+k++ will find it faster than this page will.

### Community

A **workspace** — one separate space for one group of people and all their work. Not a forum, and not a vibe. Your book club and your work team are two different communities, and neither can see the other. See [Working with communities](../guides/communities.md).

### Initiative

A **folder for one big effort** inside a community, holding that effort's tools. It's also the level where you decide who's involved, which is why it matters more than "folder" suggests: its contents are visible only to its members.

And yes, the app is called Initiative too. Context usually sorts it out. See [Working with initiatives](../guides/initiatives.md).

### Tool

Any of the nine kinds of thing that live inside an initiative: **projects**, **files**, **calendars**, **queues**, **counters**, **dashboards**, **posts**, **libraries** and **wikis**.

Worth knowing because they all behave alike — same sharing, same tags, same comment threads — so learning one teaches you the rest. The list has grown before. See [Tools](../guides/tools.md).

### Handle

Your name plus a four-digit number, written `jordan#1234`. The number is what lets two people share a name, and it's part of the handle rather than decoration — somebody typing `jordan` alone won't find you.

It's how you're identified everywhere you haven't set a [display name](../guides/communities.md#your-name-in-a-community), and it addresses your profile at `/u/jordan1234`.

### Presence

The dot under your picture: **online**, **idle**, **busy** or **offline**. Not the same as your profile **status**, which is the line about what you're up to, and not the same as a task's **status** either. Sorry. See [Saying how you're around](../account/profile-and-preferences.md#saying-how-youre-around).

### Full access

The override held by the built-in **Moderator** role: Moderators reach everything in their initiative regardless of how individual items are shared, and can change who else has access.

It's unique to Moderator. No other role can be given it, however much you'd like to. See [Initiative roles](../sharing/initiative-roles.md#the-two-built-in-roles).

### Access level

How much somebody can do with one specific project or file: **Viewer** (read), **Editor** (read and change), or **Owner** (that, plus deciding who else gets in). Separate from their initiative role, and both apply. See [Sharing projects & files](../sharing/sharing-projects-and-files.md).

### Join policy

How an initiative lets people in: **invite only** (a manager adds you), **by request** (you ask, a manager answers), or **anyone can join** (one click). See [How people join an initiative](../guides/initiatives.md#how-people-join-an-initiative).

### Guest

Somebody let into a community for a set number of days, to only the initiatives and items their invite named. A guest has a real account and can edit what they were given, takes no seat, and is removed when their time ends. See [Guests](../sharing/guests.md).

### Break-glass

A short-lived, recorded, self-issued grant that lets platform staff reach a community they don't belong to — used instead of any standing back door, and it expires on its own. See [Platform roles](../running-a-server/platform-roles.md#cross-community-access-break-glass-and-time-bound-grants).

### Archive vs. Trash

Two different safety nets, and people mix them up.

**Archive** tucks a finished thing out of sight, keeps everything, and lasts forever. **Trash** is where deleted things wait before being removed for good, for however long your admin set. Archive when the work is over; delete when it was a mistake.

### Message policy

Who may *ask* to message you: **Private** (nobody), **My communities**, or **Anyone**. New accounts start Private, so a quiet **My Messages** is expected rather than broken. Every setting ends in a request you accept or decline. See [Who can reach you](../guides/messages.md#who-can-reach-you).

### Platform role vs. community role

Two separate ladders. A **community role** (guest, member, admin or superadmin) governs one workspace. A **platform role** (member, support, moderator, operator, owner) governs the whole server.

Being an admin of your community gives you no authority over anybody else's. See [Platform roles](../running-a-server/platform-roles.md).
