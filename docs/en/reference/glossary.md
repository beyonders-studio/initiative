# Glossary

Initiative gives a handful of ordinary words specific jobs. These are the ones where guessing from the everyday meaning will send you somewhere wrong. Every other word is explained on the page that owns it, and the search box at the top of this site will find it faster than this page can.

### Community

A **workspace**: one separate space for one group of people and all of their work. It means a workspace here, rather than a forum. Your book club and your work team would be two communities, and neither can see into the other. See [Communities](../guides/communities.md).

### Initiative

A **folder for one big effort** inside a community, holding that effort's tools. It's also where you decide who's involved, because only an initiative's members see what's in it, so it matters more than "folder" suggests.

And yes, the app is called Initiative too. Context usually sorts it out. See [Initiatives](../guides/initiatives.md).

### Project

A list of **tasks**, which you can look at as a table, a board, a Gantt chart or a calendar. In everyday speech a project is the big effort, but here the big effort is the initiative, and a project is one piece of work inside it. See [Projects & tasks](../guides/projects-and-tasks.md).

### File

Anything in the Files tool. That includes uploads, and also the text documents, spreadsheets and whiteboards you write in Initiative itself, plus smart links to things kept elsewhere. See [Files](../guides/files.md).

### Tool

One of the kinds of thing that live inside an initiative: **projects**, **files**, **calendars**, **counters**, **dashboards**, **posts**, **wikis** and **contacts**. They all work alike, with the same sharing, tags and comments, so learning one teaches you most of the rest. See [Tools](../guides/tools.md).

### Handle

Your username plus a four-digit number, written `jordan#1234`. The number lets two people share a name, and it's part of the handle, so somebody typing `jordan` alone won't find you.

People see your handle wherever you haven't set a [display name](../guides/communities.md#your-name-in-a-community), and your profile lives at `/u/jordan1234`.

### Presence and status

Your **presence** is the dot on your picture: **Online**, **Idle**, **Busy** or **Offline**. Your **status** is the short line about what you're up to. A task's status is a third thing entirely, and we're sorry about that. See [Profile & preferences](../account/profile-and-preferences.md).

### Moderator

Two different jobs share this name.

In an initiative, **Moderator** is a built-in role with **Full access**: a Moderator can view and edit everything in that initiative, however each thing is shared. No other role can be given Full access. See [Initiative roles](../sharing/initiative-roles.md).

On a server, moderator is one of the staff roles, for the people who handle reports and suspensions. Being one kind of moderator doesn't make you the other.

### Owner

The **Owner** of a project, file or other tool is whoever made it. On top of changing it, they can delete it, export it and decide who else gets in, which its **Viewers** and **Editors** can't. See [Sharing](../sharing/sharing-projects-and-files.md).

The server also has an **Owner**: the top staff role, who configures the whole server. The two have nothing to do with each other.

### Admin and superadmin

In a community, an **admin** runs the place: members, invites, initiatives and settings. Its **superadmin** does all of that and also decides how people get in, including sign-in, AI and plug-ins. Only a superadmin can make somebody else one.

Both words stop at the edge of their own community. Being a superadmin gives you no say over anybody else's community, or over the server. See [Running a community](../guides/running-a-community.md).

### Community role vs. platform role

Two separate ladders. A **community role** (**Guest**, **Member**, **Admin** or **Superadmin**) covers one community. A **platform role** covers the whole server, and from the bottom up it goes **Member**, **Moderator**, **Support**, **Operator** and **Owner**. Almost everybody is a platform Member, and that's all they'll ever need to be. See [Platform roles](../running-a-server/platform-roles.md).

### Guest

Somebody let into a community for a set number of days, to only the initiatives and items their invite named. A guest has a real account and is removed when their time is up. Guests are available only where whoever runs your server has switched them on. See [Guests](../sharing/guests.md).

### Archive vs. Trash

Two different safety nets, and people mix them up.

**Archive** tucks a finished thing out of sight and keeps all of it for as long as you like. **Trash** is where deleted things wait before they're removed for good, and you can restore them from there until then. Archive when the work is over, and delete when it was a mistake.

### Private (messages)

**Private** is a message setting meaning nobody outside your connections can ask to message you. It doesn't cut you off: your connections can still message you, and anybody who knows your exact handle can still ask to connect. See [Choose who can ask to message you](../guides/messages.md#who-can-reach-you).

### Break-glass

A short grant that a server's operator or owner gives themselves to reach a community they don't belong to. It's recorded the moment it's made and expires on its own. Other staff ask for access instead, and somebody has to approve it. See [Platform roles](../running-a-server/platform-roles.md).
